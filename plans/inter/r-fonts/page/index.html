Four proposals

Three judges scored every font out of 10. One judged the back of the room, one judged students, and one judged the whole product. They agree on Inter and IBM Plex Sans. They split on Luciole, the best from far away, and Noto Sans, the students judge’s pick.

How we tested. Each font was cut down to the characters 21Board uses, then loaded into the real board at 1920 px with a Clock, Timer, Stopwatch, Name Picker, Calculator, and Notes. Back-of-the-room views shrink that screenshot to what 20/40 eyes see on a 75 inch board from 30 feet, then enlarge it. The pictures below show part of that board near full size. The samples are live, in each font as we’d build it.

Why Inter over Plex

  • Plex reads a little better from the back of the room, 7.5 to 7. It needs no build changes.
  • Inter’s letters are bigger and wider. Its x-height is 0.546 em to Plex’s 0.516, and its average letter is 0.536 em wide to 0.505. Size and width help most from far away.
  • Inter has every Calculator symbol but ∛, and weights up to 900. Plex lacks the ⁻ in 10⁻³ and the ⁿ in xⁿ, and stops at 700.
  • Inter keeps its own name, and its file is smaller, 54.5 KB to 79.9 KB.
  • Inter has the highest total of the four, 23 to Plex’s 22.

Four languages

Each window will speak English, Spanish, Russian, or Ukrainian. Tyler thought each language might need its own font. With Inter, IBM Plex Sans, or Noto Sans, one font draws all four. A Spanish Calendar beside a Russian Timer still looks like one board. Luciole has no Cyrillic, so a Russian or Ukrainian window would need a second font.

What changes for teachers

  • Text style goes away from Settings. Every board uses the new font.
  • Notes keeps its Chalkboard and Whiteboard lettering.
  • Chalkboard notes in Russian or Ukrainian use the device’s font. The chalk letters have no Cyrillic.
  • Boards saved with an old text style just open in the new font.

What the research says

  • Size matters most. Fonts with the same x-height read about the same, so a big x-height is free size (Legge and others, 2026).
  • Wider letters and more space between them help most near the limit of sight (Oderkerk and Beier, 2022; Xiong and others, 2018).
  • A two-story a holds up at a distance. A one-story a gets misread as o (Beier and Larson, 2010).
  • Open, humanist letters read faster at a glance, more so for older eyes (Dobres and others, 2016).
  • Early-reader letters made no difference for 6-year-olds, who knew both forms of a and g (Walker and Reynolds, 2003). Most of their books use the two-story g (Wong and others, 2018).
  • Dyslexia fonts don’t help. Across 15 studies and 688 readers, they had no effect (Azzarello and others, 2026). Extra spacing is what helped (Marinus and others, 2016).

Fonts we set aside

  • Fira Sans. A small minus that reads like a hyphen.
  • Readex Pro. No Cyrillic or Urdu letters. No tabular figures, so clocks jiggle.
  • Lexend. No Cyrillic or tabular figures, and its one-story a reads as o from far away.
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. No Cyrillic, and a slash through every 0 on every clock.
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible. Today’s Easier to read. The same slashed 0, with no Cyrillic or Turkish İ.
  • Andika. Today’s Early readers. Its one-story a reads as o, and it looks like a workbook.
  • Roboto. Today’s Standard on Chromebooks. I and l look alike.
  • Open Sans. Pale at 400, and I and l look alike.
  • Source Sans 3. Narrow letters and a small x-height.
  • Lato. The biggest files, and I and l look alike.
  • Nunito Sans. It needs weight 650 to look sturdy. No Greek.
  • Nunito. Light and small, with soft ends that can look young.
  • Signika. No Cyrillic. Narrow, so rn turns into m from far away.
  • Overpass. Road-sign letters, and -> turns into an arrow.
  • B612. No Cyrillic. Only 5 of 128 Latin Extended-A letters, so Dvořák breaks.
  • Inclusive Sans. No Cyrillic. A slashed 0 that stays on, and most Calculator symbols missing.
  • Ysabeau Infant. The smallest x-height tested, and the palest from far away.
  • Playpen Sans. Not tested. Its handwriting look suits Notes more than the whole board.

Questions for Tyler

  1. Go with Inter? Yes, or name another.
  2. One font for all four languages? Yes. Inter draws all four, and Cyrillic adds 9.5 KB.
  3. Add Greek and Vietnamese too? About 16 KB more for Inter. Without them, Vietnamese names mix two fonts.
  4. Use a slightly heavier regular? About 490 instead of 400, for the back of the room.
  5. Keep the two-story a? Research favors it. Inter can switch to a one-story a.
  6. Keep Georgia italic for Calculator letters? Or add Inter’s italic, about 3 KB for the Calculator’s a, b, n, and x.
