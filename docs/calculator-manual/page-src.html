Steps and Hide the answer

Steps work a problem out one part at a time, the way you would on the board.

Steps

  • Next step works out the part that’s marked and marks the next. It works before = too.
  • PEMDAS lights the letter for each step.
  • Back goes back a step. Show all writes out every step. Replay starts again.
  • A clicker works too. Page Down is Next step, and Page Up is Back.
  • The top of the canvas counts the steps. In a wide window, it names each rule too.

Hide the answer

Turn it on at the top of the canvas or in the menu. The answer waits behind a ?, so the class can predict it. Tap the ? to show it.

While it’s on, the answer doesn’t show as you type. History hides it too. The last step shows it.

    The same as Desmos

    • The answer shows as you type.
    • The order of operations. Side by side means ×, so 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2) = 9.
    • √, powers, and fractions are boxes. → leaves a box.
    • % means “% of”. 10% of 50 = 5.
    • ans shows as a small box with its number.
    • ⇄ switches an answer like 0.125 to 1/8 and back.
    • 10 digits. Past a billion, 1.07374182 × 10⁹.
    • Test practice has Desmos Four-Function’s keys and 7 digits.
    • The same typing, such as ^, sqrt, and pi.

    Different on purpose

    • × where Desmos writes ·, and = where Desmos has ↵.
    • Kind words, such as “Can’t divide by 0”, where Desmos says “undefined”.
    • One problem at a time, as on iPhone.
    • = after a lone number shows its value. A problem that starts with ×, ÷, or + works on the 0 on screen. Desmos shows no answer for either.
    • A faint comma groups the digits, as in 1,234,567. Desmos uses a narrow space.
    • Answers below 10⁻³²⁴ say “Too small to show”. Desmos shows 0.

    How the math works

    Tap an example to see its steps.

    Order of operations

    Parentheses first, then powers and roots. Then × and ÷, left to right. Then + and −, left to right.

    Boxes, as in Desmos

    √, powers, and fractions are boxes. What you type stays inside until →. +, −, and × leave a power on their own.

    Negative numbers and powers

    −3² is −9, because the power comes first. (−3)² is 9. A − right after another sign makes a negative number.

    Side by side means ×

    2π, 2(3 + 4), and 3√16 multiply. They count as ×, left to right, so 6 ÷ 2(1 + 2) = 9, as in Desmos. The a/b key writes the other reading.

    Exact answers

    The math is exact, so 0.1 + 0.2 is 0.3. Answers show 10 digits, as in Desmos. A faint comma groups the digits.

    Percent

    % means “% of”, as in Desmos, so the problem reads true as written. Tick 50 + 10% = 55 in the menu for the iPhone way.

    Repeated =

    Another = repeats the last step. Each = adds a line to History.

    Kind messages

    Desmos says “undefined”. The Calculator says what went wrong, in words a child can read, and keeps the problem on screen to fix.

    Saving

    • Each window keeps its problem, the last in History, and its menu settings.
    • Two Calculator windows keep their own work.
    • With Board Sync, your other devices get it too. If two devices change a window at once, the later change wins. Board history keeps the other.
    • Board history keeps earlier versions. Earlier versions… in the window menu brings one back.
    • Reset clears the problem, History, and settings. The window goes back to its first size.
    • A full backup keeps it all. A blank template keeps the settings, without the problem or History.
    • After a reload, the steps start over and a hidden answer hides again.
